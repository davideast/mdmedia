# The Keeper's Log

The storm had been circling the island for three days when the boy finally
climbed the stairs. Mara heard him long before she saw him: every step on the
iron spiral rang like a struck bell.

**ELI**: Are you the keeper? They said in the village you'd be up here.

**MARA**: They say a lot of things in the village. Shut the hatch. You're
letting the weather in.

**ELI** *(out of breath)*: Sorry. Sorry. It's just, I've been climbing for ages.
How many steps is it?

**MARA**: Two hundred and twelve. You'll count them yourself on the way down.

### The lamp

She turned back to the great lens, polishing a brass fitting that was already
spotless. The boy hovered at the edge of the light.

**ELI**: My mother says you keep a log. Every ship, every night, for forty years.
She says—

**MARA** *(cutting him off, sharp)*: Your mother talks too much.

For a long moment the only sound was the rain against the glass, and the slow
grinding turn of the lamp.

**MARA** *(softer, almost to herself)*: Which ship was his?

**ELI**: The *Wren*. Nineteen years ago. It never came back.

**MARA** *(whispering)*: I know the night. I wrote it down.

1.5s

She set the cloth aside, crossed to the shelf, and pulled down a book whose
spine had split along its length.

**MARA**: Sit. If you want the truth, you'll have to hear all of it. And you
won't like the ending.

**ELI** *(barely audible)*: I didn't come up here to like it.
