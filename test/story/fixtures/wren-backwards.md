# Wren Backwards

### The Antique Shop

```music
A whimsical, dusty music box melody played on creaky harpsichord and cello.
```

*(whimsical, introductory)* Great-Aunt Odile's shop in Gullmouth sold old things, and most of the old things were mirrors. There were round mirrors and tall mirrors, mirrors in gold frames and mirrors in frames carved like seaweed. There was a mirror shaped like a moon and a mirror so foggy it only showed shapes. Some of them had sheets draped over them, and Odile never said why.

*(droll, listing mishaps)* Wren Pickett had been there for six days, and she had already broken a teapot, tracked mud up the stairs, and laughed at the wrong moment four times. It was the laugh that was the problem. Wren's laugh didn't come out like other people's laughs. It came out like a goose being stepped on.

**WREN** *(uncontrollable, jarring goose-honk)*: HONK.

*(building tension, observant)* On the sixth day, a lady came into the shop to sell a mirror, and the lady's hat had a whole stuffed pigeon on it, and the pigeon was looking right at Wren. Wren held her breath. She pressed her lips together. She thought about sad things.

**WREN** *(sudden, mortifying burst)*: HONK.

*(dryly matter-of-fact)* The lady left without selling the mirror.

**ODILE** *(sharp, stern)*: Wren Pickett.

*(dry, brisk)* Banging her cane on the floor.

**ODILE** *(curt, commanding)*: Upstairs.

*(observant, quiet)* Wren stomped up the stairs. At the top of the landing hung the tallest mirror in the shop, in a black frame carved with little fish. Wren stuck her tongue out at it. Her reflection stuck its tongue out back. A beat late.

### Midnight Meeting

```music
Eerie, quiet moonlight ambience with soft mysterious strings and ticking clock.
```

*(quiet, restless)* That night she couldn't sleep. The house creaked. The sea hushed against the harbour wall. At midnight, she crept out to the landing, because she had to know.

*(expectant, tense)* The reflection was waiting for her.

*(eerie, observant)* It looked exactly like Wren, except it didn't. Its copper bob was smooth instead of frizzy. Its raincoat was buttoned all the way to the top. Its parting was on the wrong side. And its eyes were silver, catching the moonlight like coins.

*(chilling, still)* It smiled. Wren wasn't smiling.

**NERW** *(sweetly polite, unnerving)*: Hello, Wren.

*(disturbed)* Its voice was Wren's voice, but sweeter.

**NERW** *(smooth, gentle)*: I'm Nerw.

**WREN** *(suspicious, defensive)*: That's my name backwards.

**NERW** *(playful, chilling)*: I'm you backwards.

*(watching closely)* Nerw tilted its head.

**NERW** *(matter-of-fact, eerie)*: I've been copying you for eleven years. Every frown. Every stomp. Every honk.

*(repulsed)* It shuddered a little.

**NERW** *(softly probing)*: Don't you ever get tired of being you?

*(heavy with fatigue)* Wren thought about the teapot, and the mud, and the pigeon lady.

**WREN** *(quiet, defeated)*: Sometimes.

**NERW** *(persuasive, enticing)*: Then let's swap. Just for one day. You come in here and have a rest, and I'll go out there and be you. I'll be very good at it.

*(beckoning)* Nerw put its palm flat against the glass.

**NERW** *(prismatically sweet, soothing)*: A holiday from being Wren. Wouldn't that be nice?

*(tempted, longing)* It would, Wren thought. It really would.

### Behind the Glass

```music
Surreal, backwards-reversing piano notes and chilling, silvery chime ambience.
```

*(disoriented, bewildered)* When Wren opened her eyes, she was on the landing, but the landing was wrong. Everything was silver. The wallpaper was silver, the stairs were silver, the moonlight was silver. The clock on the wall ran backwards, ticking anticlockwise. The sign on the bathroom door said HTAB. And outside the window, the rain was falling up.

*(helpless, surreal)* On the other side of the mirror, Nerw stood on the real landing, in the real house, and buttoned the top button of the real raincoat.

**WREN** *(panicked, shocked)*: Nerw!

*(desperate, urgent)* Wren pounded on the glass.

**WREN** *(frightened, pleading)*: Nerw, this is weird, I don't like it, let me back.

**NERW** *(unnervingly polite, sweet)*: Tomorrow. Sleep tight.

*(indifferent, dismissive)* And it walked away.

**GILBERT** *(quiet, tentative)*: Psst.

*(startled, marveling)* Wren spun around. On the silver stairs sat a small, threadbare rabbit. He had one button eye and one eye that was a stitched cross, and one of his ears was held on with a safety pin.

**WREN** *(awestruck, breathless)*: Gilbert?

*(wistful, nostalgic)* She'd lost Gilbert at the seaside when she was five. She'd cried for a week.

**GILBERT** *(gentle, soft-spoken)*: I'm his reflection. When you lose a thing, its reflection stays behind the glass. Waiting.

*(emotional, quiet)* He wiped his eye with a paw.

**GILBERT** *(tender, aching)*: I've been waiting a very long time. Hello, Wren.

### The Perfect Swap

```music
A bittersweet, longing oboe and muted strings reflecting isolation.
```

*(observant, quiet)* In the morning, Wren and Gilbert watched the real house through the mirrors.

*(watching closely, uneasy)* Nerw came down to breakfast at seven sharp, with its hair brushed and its raincoat on. It made the tea. It said please and thank you. It laughed at Great-Aunt Odile's joke, a small, tinkly laugh like a teaspoon on a cup.

**ODILE** *(surprised, evaluating)*: Well.

*(watching over her loupe)* Looking at it over her loupe.

**ODILE** *(thoughtful, sharp)*: Well, well.

*(attentive, quiet)* Through the shop window, Wren watched Nerw sweep the step. Pim from the fish shop next door came out with a bucket.

*(pensive, insecure)* Pim was the only one in Gullmouth who'd ever laughed at Wren's honk. Wren had never worked out whether he was laughing with her or at her, and it bothered her a lot.

**PIM** *(cheerful, jovial)*: Morning. Did you hear about the fish who couldn't count? He kept losing his plaice.

*(amused despite herself)* Wren, behind the glass, snorted.

*(observing)* Nerw just smiled politely.

**NERW** *(prismatically sweet, unnervingly polite)*: That's very clever, Pim.

*(puzzled)* Pim looked at Nerw for a long moment. Then he went back inside.

*(heartbroken, feeling replaced)* All day it was like that. Nerw was perfect. Nerw was polite. Nerw didn't break anything. And everybody seemed to like Nerw better, and Wren, watching from the silver side, felt smaller and smaller.

### Race to the Fish Shop

```music
Urgent, rushing woodwinds and staccato percussion with swirling wind tones.
```

*(breathless, desperate)* They ran.

*(frantic, overwhelmed)* Through the silver streets of backwards Gullmouth, where the shop signs said YREKAB and STEEWS, where the gulls flew tail first, where the rain fell up into their faces. Mirrors went dark around them one after another, like someone blowing out candles.

*(panicked, tense)* They reached the fish shop window just as Nerw did, on the other side.

*(chilled, ominous)* Nerw stood on the pavement in front of the glass with a sheet in its hands. Pim was behind it in the shop doorway.

**NERW** *(cold, triumphant)*: Too late.

**GILBERT** *(sharp, urgent)*: No. Remember the rule. A reflection has to copy you, if you're standing in front of the glass.

*(suddenly resolute)* Wren understood.

*(determined, focused)* She stood right in front of Nerw. She raised her hand. Nerw, unwillingly, raised its hand. Wren stamped her foot. Nerw stamped its foot.

**NERW** *(venomous, spiteful)*: You can't make me do anything I can't do. I'm better at being you than you are.

*(startled, amused)* And just then, behind Nerw, Pim leaned out of the doorway with a huge rubbery fish in each hand, and held them up on either side of his head, like ears.

**PIM** *(cheeky, upbeat)*: What do you call a fish with no eyes? A FSH.

*(caught off guard, delighted)* Wren laughed.

*(overwhelmed with laughter)* It came up from her toes. It came out like a goose being stepped on by another goose. It was the loudest, honkiest, most embarrassing laugh she had ever laughed.

**WREN** *(explosive, hysterical)*: HONK.

*(straining, pathetic)* And Nerw tried. It opened its mouth and tried to copy it. But all that came out was a small, tinkly laugh like a teaspoon on a cup.

*(shocked, dramatic)* The glass cracked straight down the middle.
