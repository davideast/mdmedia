# The Most Embarrassing Day of My Life

### 7:43 a.m.

```music
playful, bouncy middle school comedy theme, pizzicato strings, light drums, ukulele; a morning full of mischief
```

By Friday afternoon, I was going to be the most popular kid at Westfield Middle
School.

By Friday afternoon, I was also going to lose my best friend.

But at 7:43 that morning, I didn't know either of those things.

I was too busy trying to get a Pop-Tart out of a vending machine.

**MILO** *(straining)*: Come on. Come on!

I slammed my palm against the glass.

The Pop-Tart hung from its metal coil, halfway to freedom.

**JUNE** *(amused)*: You're negotiating with a vending machine again?

I turned. June Park stood behind me, holding two chocolate milks.

**MILO**: It's not negotiating. It's psychological warfare.

**JUNE**: You're losing.

**MILO** *(with fake dignity)*: I'm establishing dominance.

June reached around me, pressed the refund button, and the Pop-Tart dropped.

I stared at it. She handed me a chocolate milk.

**JUNE** *(deadpan)*: You're welcome, General.

That was June. My best friend since third grade. She was smart, sarcastic, and
possibly the only person alive who could beat me at Mario Kart while eating a
bowl of cereal.

She was also the reason I was about to make the dumbest decision of my thirteen
years on Earth.

I just didn't know it yet.

### First period

```music
bright school-morning fanfare, upbeat marimba and claps, announcement energy
```

The announcement came during first period.

**HARPER** *(over the school loudspeaker)*: Good morning, Westfield Wildcats!

The voice belonged to Harper Blake, eighth grader, student council president,
and a person so popular that people actually moved out of her way in the
hallway.

**HARPER** *(over the loudspeaker)*: As you know, today is our annual Friday
Showcase! And this year, we'll be choosing a brand-new host for Wildcat Weekly!

I sat straight up.

Wildcat Weekly was our school's Friday video announcement show. Whoever hosted
it got to interview students, make jokes, and appear on every television in the
building.

I'd been dreaming about that job since sixth grade.

Mostly because, in sixth grade, I'd accidentally dumped an entire carton of
orange juice down my pants at lunch.

People had called me Juice Boy for seven months.

I needed a rebrand.

**HARPER** *(over the loudspeaker)*: Finalists, remember to submit your audition
reels by lunch. We'll announce the winner at this afternoon's assembly!

I turned to June. She smiled.

**JUNE**: You're going to win.

**MILO**: You think?

**JUNE**: Absolutely. You're the funniest person I know.

**MILO**: Thank you.

**JUNE** *(teasing)*: Which is honestly pretty concerning.

I grinned. Then my phone buzzed. A message from Harper.

**HARPER** *(a text message, breezy)*: Hey! Saw your audition. Really funny.

My stomach did a backflip.

**MILO** *(thrilled)*: Thanks!!

Three dots appeared.

**HARPER** *(a text message)*: One thing. We're adding a comedy montage to the
assembly. Got any funny clips? Something that would really get people laughing?

I thought about it. I had plenty of funny videos. Mostly of myself falling off
things.

But there was one video. One spectacularly funny video.

And it wasn't mine.

### Two weeks earlier

```music
sneaky, quirky flashback cue, muted trumpet and bassoon, a little goofy, bedroom dance energy
```

Two weeks earlier, June had sent me a recording.

She'd been secretly practicing for the spring talent show. A dance routine. To
a song she'd written herself.

And let me tell you something about June Park. She was an incredible dancer.

But when she got nervous, something happened to her arms that could only be
described as inflatable car dealership man.

The video began with June wearing purple sunglasses in her bedroom.

**JUNE** *(to her phone camera, nervous but determined)*: Okay. This is take
seventeen. If this is bad, I'm joining the chess club.

Then the music started.

She spun. She slid. She attempted a move that looked like a malfunctioning
helicopter.

And then... crash. She disappeared behind her bed.

For three seconds, nothing happened.

Then one hand emerged from the floor and gave a thumbs-up.

I'd laughed so hard I couldn't breathe.

But June had made me promise something.

**JUNE** *(serious, a little scared)*: Nobody sees this, Milo. Seriously. I'm
already terrified of performing in front of people.

**MILO**: Cross my heart.

**JUNE**: And don't send it to anyone.

**MILO**: I won't.

She'd studied my face.

**JUNE** *(quietly)*: Promise?

**MILO** *(sincere)*: Promise.

Standing in the hallway now, I opened our messages.

The thumbnail stared back at me. Purple sunglasses. Helicopter arms. The
funniest twenty-three seconds ever recorded.

I could practically hear the assembly laughing. I could picture Harper
congratulating me. I could picture everyone finally forgetting Juice Boy.

My thumb hovered over the video.

```pause
1s
```

Then I forwarded it.

**MILO** *(typing, trying to sound casual)*: This one's pretty funny.

Harper responded almost immediately.

**HARPER** *(a text message, ecstatic)*: Oh. My. God. Milo. This is gold.

I smiled.

Then my stomach tightened. Just a little.

### Lunch

```music
cheerful cafeteria bustle cue that slowly turns uneasy, light percussion, a wrong note creeping in
```

By lunch, things were going better than I'd imagined.

Harper approached our table.

**HARPER** *(delighted)*: Milo! Dude!

She gave me a high five. An actual high five. From Harper Blake. At our lunch
table.

Across from me, June nearly choked on a french fry.

**HARPER**: You're definitely one of my favorites. That clip? Hilarious. We're
using it in the assembly.

My smile froze.

**MILO** *(uneasy)*: Wait. Using it?

**HARPER** *(walking away, breezy)*: Yeah! Right before we announce the new
host. It's going to kill.

She walked away. June looked at me.

**JUNE**: What clip?

I stared at my tray.

**JUNE** *(sharper)*: Milo?

I tried to laugh. It sounded like a lawn mower failing to start.

**MILO** *(nervous laugh)*: Okay. Funny story.

June didn't smile.

**JUNE** *(each word separate)*: What. Clip.

I swallowed.

**MILO** *(small)*: The dancing one.

She blinked. For one second, I thought she'd laugh.

Instead, her face changed. Not angry. Worse. Hurt.

**JUNE** *(stunned, hurt)*: You sent my video?

**MILO**: It was just to Harper.

**JUNE**: You promised.

**MILO**: I know, but—

**JUNE** *(cutting him off, hurt)*: You promised, Milo.

Around us, the cafeteria continued its usual chaos. Someone launched a grape
across the room.

**KID** *(loud and indignant, far across the cafeteria)*: Ketchup is TOO a vegetable!

But at our table, everything had gone quiet.

**MILO** *(ashamed)*: I thought it might help me win.

June stared at me. Then she picked up her tray.

**MILO** *(pleading)*: June, come on.

She stopped.

**JUNE** *(quiet)*: You know why I sent you that video?

**MILO**: To see if the dance was good.

**JUNE**: No.

Her voice shook.

**JUNE** *(voice shaking)*: Because I was scared. And you always make scary
things feel less scary.

She looked down.

**JUNE** *(barely holding it together)*: I guess that was stupid.

She walked away.

It was probably the worst french fry of my entire life.

### 1:45 p.m.

```music
ticking-clock tension cue, pizzicato strings and a nervous clarinet, comedic suspense
```

At 1:45, I had forty-five minutes to save my friendship.

Unfortunately, I had absolutely no idea how.

I found Harper backstage in the auditorium, checking her notes.

**MILO** *(nervous)*: Harper. Hey. Quick question.

**HARPER** *(friendly)*: What's up, future TV star?

**MILO**: About that video.

**HARPER**: So funny, right?

**MILO**: Yeah, but the thing is—

My throat went dry. I imagined telling her the truth.

Hey, Harper. That video I sent you? I wasn't supposed to. My best friend
trusted me, and I used it to get your attention.

Then I imagined her face. Her disappointment. The hosting job disappearing.

**MILO** *(backing down)*: You know what? Never mind.

**HARPER**: Actually, could you help check the montage? The tech club loaded
everything already.

I glanced at the screen. There it was. A folder labeled Friday Showcase Final.
Inside was a file called June Dance Final.

My heart sank.

I had two choices. Tell Harper the truth. Or somehow delete the video.

I looked around. Nobody was watching.

I walked to the laptop. Clicked the file. Dragged it toward the trash. And...

**TECHKID** *(shouting, startled)*: What are you doing?

I jumped. My elbow hit a plastic cup sitting beside the keyboard. The cup
tipped. Water spread across the table. The laptop screen went black.

```pause
1.5s
```

For a moment, the world stopped.

Then, from somewhere behind the curtain, a voice yelled:

**ALVAREZ** *(yelling from offstage)*: Why is the projector dead?

Oh. Oh, this was bad.

### 2:25 p.m.

```music
big auditorium buildup, marching snare and brass swelling, chaotic and anxious, then stopping short
```

At 2:25, the auditorium was packed.

The projector still wasn't working. Mr. Alvarez, our technology teacher, was
crouched behind the stage muttering words that definitely weren't in the school
handbook.

Harper was pacing. The principal was looking at his watch.

And I was sitting in the front row, wondering whether changing my name and
moving to Canada was a realistic option.

Then I saw June. Third row. Sitting alone.

She noticed me. Looked away.

My stomach hurt. Not because I was nervous about winning anymore.

Because I realized that even if I did win, June wouldn't be sitting beside me
afterward. And somehow, that made the whole thing feel pointless.

At 2:29, the projector flickered back to life. The auditorium cheered. Mr.
Alvarez raised both arms like he'd just won the Super Bowl.

Harper stepped onto the stage.

**HARPER** *(into a microphone, hyping the crowd)*: All right, Wildcats! Who's
ready for our Friday Showcase?

The crowd erupted. She introduced the first video. Then the second. Then...

**HARPER** *(into the microphone)*: Before we announce our next Wildcat Weekly
host, we've got one last hilarious clip!

My blood turned to ice.

A purple pair of sunglasses appeared on the giant screen. June's face. Twenty
feet tall.

I heard someone laugh.

**KID** *(laughing, from the crowd)*: Hey! That's June!

The video froze before it started. Because I'd stood up.

**MILO** *(shouting)*: Stop!

```pause

```

Three hundred heads turned toward me.

**HARPER** *(confused)*: Milo?

I climbed onto the stage. I had no plan. No clever joke. No idea what I was
doing.

But I knew one thing. I wasn't going to sit there and watch my best friend get
laughed at because of me.

**MILO** *(shaky)*: Please don't play that video.

Harper lowered the microphone.

**HARPER**: Why?

I looked into the audience. June was staring at me. My mouth went dry.

**MILO**: Because...

I took a breath.

**MILO** *(steadier)*: Because I sent it without permission.

A few people whispered.

**MILO**: It was a private video. My friend trusted me with it, and I sent it to
Harper because I thought it would help me win.

Harper looked stunned.

**HARPER** *(stunned)*: Milo, I didn't know—

**MILO** *(gently)*: I know. It's not your fault.

I turned toward the audience.

**MILO** *(to the whole auditorium)*: I wanted everyone to think I was funny. And
cool. And not...

I stopped. Then laughed at myself.

**MILO** *(laughing at himself)*: Not the kid who spilled orange juice down his
pants in sixth grade.

Somebody shouted.

**KID** *(heckling from the crowd)*: Juice Boy!

A few students laughed. I felt my face getting hot.

**MILO** *(embarrassed)*: Yeah. That guy.

I looked down.

**MILO** *(quiet, honest)*: But I think I might've been cooler when I was just
Juice Boy. At least back then I wasn't a terrible friend.

The auditorium went quiet. I handed Harper the microphone.

**MILO** *(softly)*: I'm sorry.

And I walked offstage.

### After the assembly

```music
gentle reflective cue, soft acoustic guitar and piano, a little sad but hopeful
```

I didn't win. Priya Shah won.

Which was fair. Her audition was genuinely excellent, and unlike me, she had not
nearly electrocuted a laptop trying to cover up a betrayal.

After the assembly, I sat on the steps outside the gym.

I was considering whether Canada had decent middle schools when someone sat
beside me.

June.

For a while, neither of us said anything.

Then she handed me something. A Pop-Tart.

I looked at it.

**MILO**: Where'd you get this?

**JUNE**: The vending machine.

**MILO**: How?

**JUNE** *(dry)*: I pressed the button like a normal person.

I laughed. She didn't.

**JUNE** *(firm)*: I'm still mad at you.

**MILO**: I know.

**JUNE**: Like, really mad.

**MILO**: I know.

**JUNE**: And just because you did the right thing at the end doesn't mean what
you did before was okay.

I nodded.

**MILO** *(sincere)*: You're right.

She peeled open her chocolate milk. A few seconds passed.

**JUNE** *(hesitant)*: Did you really think my dance was funny?

I looked at her.

**MILO** *(fond, grinning)*: June. You fell behind a bed and gave a thumbs-up
like you'd survived an avalanche.

Her mouth twitched.

**JUNE** *(fighting a smile)*: That was artistic.

**MILO**: It was historic.

She tried not to laugh. Failed. Then she got serious again.

**JUNE** *(serious)*: I was going to quit the talent show.

**MILO**: What?

**JUNE**: After lunch. I figured if people were going to laugh at me anyway, why
bother?

I looked down.

**MILO** *(quiet)*: I'm sorry.

**JUNE**: I know.

She pulled out her phone.

**JUNE** *(a little shy)*: I practiced a new ending.

**MILO** *(surprised)*: What?

**JUNE**: For the dance. Want to see it?

**MILO**: Really?

**JUNE** *(mock stern)*: Yeah. But there are rules.

**MILO**: Anything.

**JUNE**: No sharing.

**MILO**: Obviously.

**JUNE**: No screenshots.

**MILO**: Never.

**JUNE** *(deadpan)*: And if I fall down, you are legally required to pretend it
was choreography.

I nodded solemnly.

**MILO** *(solemn)*: Understood.

She pressed play.

On the screen, June spun across her bedroom. She landed. She jumped. She
executed a move I'd never seen before.

Then she stepped backward onto a skateboard. Her arms shot into the air. She
disappeared behind the bed.

Crash.

```pause
1.5s
```

Silence.

Then one hand rose from the floor. Thumbs-up.

I started laughing. So did she.

And this time, I didn't reach for the share button.

### Three weeks later

```music
triumphant feel-good finale, full band with horns and handclaps, joyful talent show energy, warm ending
```

Three weeks later, June Park performed at the Westfield Talent Show.

She wore purple sunglasses. She danced like a maniac. And she absolutely
crushed it.

The whole auditorium cheered. I cheered louder than anyone.

Afterward, she found me backstage.

**JUNE** *(out of breath, eager)*: Well?

I pretended to think.

**MILO** *(teasing)*: Hmm. Six out of ten.

She punched my arm.

**MILO** *(yelping, laughing)*: Ow! Kidding! Eleven! Twelve! A million!

She grinned. Then she looked toward the auditorium.

**JUNE** *(softer)*: Hey, Milo?

**MILO**: Yeah?

**JUNE** *(sincere)*: Thanks for being there.

I smiled.

**MILO**: Wouldn't have missed it.

She started walking away. Then stopped.

**JUNE**: Oh, and Milo?

**MILO**: Yeah?

**JUNE** *(casual, sly)*: Next Friday, I'm auditioning for Wildcat Weekly.

I stared at her.

**MILO** *(shocked)*: You?

She shrugged.

**JUNE**: Turns out being onstage isn't so scary.

I grinned.

**MILO**: Want help making an audition video?

She narrowed her eyes.

**JUNE** *(dry)*: You're not touching the camera.

**MILO**: Fair.

**JUNE**: But...

She smiled.

**JUNE** *(warm)*: I could use a cohost.

And just like that, I realized something.

I'd spent months wanting the entire school to know my name.

But somewhere along the way, I'd almost lost the one person who'd always been
happy to say it.

**JUNE** *(shouting, far away)*: Milo!

**MILO**: What?

**JUNE** *(calling)*: Are you coming or not?

I grabbed my backpack.

**MILO** *(shouting happily)*: Coming!

And as I ran after her, I passed the vending machine.

A Pop-Tart hung crookedly from its coil. Halfway down. Stuck.

I stopped. Looked at it.

Then kept walking.

Some battles just weren't worth winning.
