## Story diagnosis after user review

The ending compresses the absent brother, discovery, confinement, rescue and lookalike into 49 seconds. Twenty editorial shots are carrying several scenes' worth of new information. The audience gets labels and outcomes with too little motivation, emotional context or visible response.

- The memorial gets two lines. We name Sebastian without adequately establishing the loss, what Bruno believes, or why Heidi cannot let go.
- Pawprints are weak evidence here: the accepted opening already featured Otto's muddy prints. A fresh insert alone does not distinguish a meaningful new clue from ordinary household mud.
- Recognition immediately becomes confinement. The audience has little opportunity to understand Heidi's discovery before a different problem takes over.
- Confinement resolves through a cut and retrospective explanation. Nobody hears the alarm, chooses to help, or visibly reaches Heidi. Simplifying difficult generated action removed the causal middle of the event.
- The audience learns Sebastian is supposedly dead only shortly before seeing the lookalike. There is little established belief for the reveal to overturn.
- Shot validity, complete utterances and declared exposition facts passed checks. Those results did not establish dramatic comprehension. The user rejected the storytelling, which supersedes the earlier automated editorial judgment.
